# Destination travel cinematic

SI-assisted: generated with Codex; pending human review.

Atmosphere WebGL compatibility: the GLES browser variant uses six fixed scattering samples and an explicit light-direction vector, without the URP lighting-feature include or camera-depth sampling/reconstruction. Desktop retains depth occlusion. The browser uses the analytic planet intersection for occlusion, so haze over nearby foreground details is approximate. The shader/pass remains explicitly referenced by the scene and marked SRPDefaultUnlit. If the device reports the shader unsupported, the component logs the graphics backend and disables the atmosphere shell rather than displaying a magenta sphere. The actual shader was present and compiled for gles3 in the inspected build log; stripping was not confirmed as the cause. A fresh WebGL build and browser-console verification are required after this change.

Window refresh: the feed camera renders at a lower depth than Main Camera, so its RenderTexture is ready before the glass is drawn. Its pose updates in LateUpdate after orbit movement. It resumes during RETURNING TO CABIN (under the transition fade), rather than waiting until the entire cinematic has ended. Skipping also refreshes the window in the same frame before camera rendering. The circular porthole uses a square 768x768 feed without the prior rectangular aspect stretch.

Rocket exhaust uses Local particle simulation, looping, zero gravity, disabled velocity inheritance and Always Simulate culling. Particles remain attached to their nozzle during translation and rotation instead of forming world-space streaks. Emitters are positioned at the lower nozzle openings. Throttle controls emission, speed, size and lifetime. Restart checks isEmitting rather than isPlaying so the engine can reignite while shutdown particles are still fading. At zero throttle, new emission stops and existing local particles finish their lifetime; skipping clears them immediately.

The exterior camera now orbits to Earth's opposite viewing side during the last 60% of the flight. Observation offsets use the radial direction from the planet center (independent of the artist's landing-marker rotation), with a 5-unit outward component. Direction interpolation follows an arc at a preserved distance from the rocket. Each camera position is pushed outside an expanded planetary bounding sphere with a 5% / minimum 1.25-unit margin for voxel steps and camera near-plane clearance. The camera then looks back at the rocket. This guard applies to Earth, Moon and Mars; landing marker transforms are unchanged. Validate exterior camera clearance exercises inside/outside corrections and orbit radius in Unity.

## Scene landing markers

EarthLanding, MoonLanding, MarsLanding and IssLanding in SampleScene now take priority over all computed landing locations. Their Transform references are serialized on DestinationTravel and discovered by name if missing. Move them to change the landing ground/feet position; rotate them to control the complete final orientation, including yaw. The green local Y axis is the surface normal. Landing Clearance (default 0.9) offsets the rocket center above the marker along that axis; set it to zero if placing a marker at the rocket center instead of the ground. Marker coordinates are converted from world space into Exterior Travel Stage local space, including when the markers are outside the stage hierarchy. Startup, normal arrival, skipping, window view, and subsequent departure all use the marker pose. The automatic Earth land bake runs only when EarthLanding is absent. Validate landing markers checks all four position/rotation conversions in Unity.

SampleScene uses its existing Main Camera and a DestinationTravel component on FireSimulation. Changing destination through SetDestination or incoming gravity starts a journey from the last arrived destination. Startup stays in the cabin with no unnecessary journey.

Sequence: move toward the cabin window, transition through a short fade to the exterior rocket, ignite three exhaust plumes, follow an arcing flight, show arrival with the destination behind the rocket, shut down the engines, and return through the window transition to the saved cabin view. The camera uses the same object throughout; no second AudioListener is added. Orbit controls are restored afterward. The experiment continues updating during the journey.

SampleScene contains a saved Exterior Travel Stage root at X=300, with Earth, Moon, Mars, ISS and RocketShip children visible in the Hierarchy and Scene View. TravelModelInstance previews each model in Edit mode and uses the same geometry at runtime. During Play, layers 29 (rocket) and 30 (exterior) isolate the cinematic: the cabin camera excludes them, the exterior shot sees only them, and the window camera sees only layer 30. These unnamed slots are unused in the current project. The exterior stays active for the window feed. Fire Simulation > Show exterior stage frames the models in Scene View; Show cabin returns the editor view to the laboratory.

TravelModels contains reduced-detail OBJ meshes and palette textures derived from the five VOX files. The original models are preserved. New edits to VOX files do not automatically regenerate these cinematic meshes. The imported meshes are centered, normalized, and Y-up.

Default flight time is 12 seconds, plus cabin departure, ignition, arrival and return shots (about 18 seconds total). Flight Seconds and Ignition Seconds are editable on DestinationTravel. Routes and sizes are illustrative; the animation does not represent orbital mechanics, true interplanetary distances or surface landing physics.

Flight now follows one parabola from the saved arrival point to the next destination, rather than three stitched sections. Quintic time easing makes both velocity and acceleration approach zero at the endpoints. Attitude transitions preserve the departure pose and finish upright at planetary arrival. Thrust joins smoothly between ignition, flight and shutdown.

Hold E continuously for one second during any part of the journey to skip to arrival and restore the cabin camera and controls. A bottom-center progress bar shows the hold percentage. Releasing E or losing application focus resets the hold. Skipping clears engine particles and commits the destination and parked pose, including when used during departure or cabin return. A queued destination remains pending.

The cabin window uses a 1024x512 RenderTexture from a separate camera mounted at the rocket's cabin position, looking outward along its orientation with a slight downward angle. It shows the local ground/horizon from the vessel, not a whole-planet orbital portrait. A small positional parallax follows the player's movement inside the laboratory. The rocket is excluded to avoid clipping its external hull across its own window. This camera has no AudioListener, excludes the cabin to prevent recursion, and pauses during the exterior cinematic. The original procedural view remains a fallback.

Earth has a blue atmospheric scattering shell plus a separate cloud mesh made from clusters of axis-aligned voxel blocks. The clouds orbit slowly as a group and use flat, opaque shaded faces; the former smooth transparent cloud sphere has been removed. The original VOX files are unchanged. Mars has a thinner warm dust-colored shell; Moon has no atmospheric halo. Atmospheric thickness/density are magnified for legibility. Shaders are serialized on the planet objects and cinematic component. Rendering still needs verification in Unity.

Earth landing uses a baked triangle center with green land UVs on the imported Earth mesh, chosen toward a broad continental interior around northern Africa rather than the former northern coastal point. Bind travel assets refreshes the point using readable imported mesh data. The anchor, rocket and arrival camera use the same local outward normal. Normal arrival and skipping use this land point. The default point is checked against a green triangle in the generated Earth OBJ. This is an illustrative touchdown location, not surveyed landing terrain.

Planet display radii use one scale derived from the user's approximate diameter reference: Earth 12,756 km maps to radius 90; Mars uses 6,771/12,756 (53.08%) and Moon 3,474/12,756 (27.23%). The three TravelModelInstance components have Use Planet Proportions enabled, preventing their Visual Scale fields from drifting independently. Their normalized source meshes all have diameter 2. Rocket height remains 1.6 display units and the ISS span 2.9; the spacecraft/planet relationship remains compressed for legibility.

Planetary Overview Strength controls a smooth wider shot around the middle of the flight. It exposes planet silhouettes and their size differences, which close-to-ground horizon shots obscure. The shot returns to the close camera before landing; zero disables the wider shot. Landing markers and their rotations are unchanged.

The exterior layout, stars, camera far clipping plane and engine plumes are adjusted for the larger planets. Planet endpoints sit over the north pole with clearance for the smaller rocket. A close spacecraft view shows the larger horizon behind it rather than fitting the whole planet into the frame. During the final 35% of the flight, the rocket smoothly rotates nose-up and engines-down. The ISS retains its docking heading.

The final position and rotation are stored as ParkedPosition and ParkedRotation at arrival. The next journey starts from that exact pose throughout engine ignition, then blends into the new flight heading only after lift-off begins. Engines scale with the rocket and decrease thrust without a power jump at arrival.

During a journey, another destination replaces the pending request. The current journey finishes and returns to the cabin, then the latest pending destination starts the next journey. Repeating the same destination does not restart a trip. The window updates only when arrival is reached. Disabling the component restores the camera and cleans up generated models and materials.

Fire Simulation > Bind travel assets repairs imported OBJ/palette references if needed. Fire Simulation > Validate travel routes checks all twelve directed route endpoints and travel references. Save the scene after binding. A new WebGL build is required to include this sequence and its referenced models. The launcher is unchanged.
